# RoboRev — Polish, Harden, and Extend

## Context

You are working on RoboRev, a full-stack AI-powered revenue management platform for multifamily real estate. The core engine (three-layer architecture, 14 database tables, 156 units of seed data, 203 passing tests, 22 API endpoints, 30+ frontend components) is built and functional.

**The problem:** We over-indexed on the engine and under-indexed on the evaluator's experience. A case study evaluator will spend 5–10 minutes with this. They'll log in, click around, run one diagnostic, and form an impression. That impression is shaped by: can I understand what this is telling me? Does it feel trustworthy? Does the person who built this understand how operators actually work?

This prompt covers three categories of work, in priority order:

1. **Submission-critical fixes** — things that undermine trust or correctness if left as-is
2. **Experience layer** — making the 5-minute evaluator walkthrough feel polished and intuitive
3. **Feature extensions** — CSV import, config templates, AI chat, PDF export

Read CLAUDE.md, ROBOREV-BUILD-CONTEXT.md, and the decisions log before starting. Update progress.md and decisions.md as you work, per existing protocol.

## Category 1: Submission-Critical Fixes

### 1A. Fix Claude Scoring Determinism

**Problem:** Layer 3 uses Claude to generate health scores (0–100). Same inputs can produce meaningfully different scores on re-runs. An evaluator who runs the diagnostic twice and gets 28 then 42 for B1 will not trust the platform.

**Fix:** Add a deterministic scoring rubric to the Claude diagnosis prompt that constrains scores to ranges based on flag combinations. Claude picks within the range and provides justification, but cannot produce a score outside the range.

Scoring rubric to embed in the diagnosis prompt:

```
SCORING RULES (you MUST follow these — scores outside the prescribed range are invalid):

- 2+ CRITICAL flags AND occupancy below crisis threshold → score 15–30
- 1 CRITICAL flag OR 3+ HIGH flags → score 30–50  
- Multiple HIGH + MEDIUM flags, no CRITICAL → score 45–60
- Mix of MEDIUM flags, possibly 1 HIGH → score 55–70
- Mostly LOW/POSITIVE flags, minor concerns only → score 70–85
- All metrics healthy, no action needed → score 85–95

Pick a specific score within the range. Justify your choice with the 2-3 most influential flags. 
Re-runs on identical inputs should produce scores within ±5 points of each other.
```

Expected outcomes after fix:
- A1 (3 flags, all low): 75–85
- A2 (7 flags, HIGH severity): 50–65
- B1 (12 flags, 2 CRITICAL): 20–35
- B2 (7 flags, zero CRITICAL, puzzle profile): 55–65

**Verification:** Run the diagnostic 3 times on the same property without changing data. Scores for each unit type must stay within a ±5 band across all 3 runs.

### 1B. Fix Competitive Landscape Table

**Problem:** The ROBOREV-ROADMAP.md competitive landscape table lists "RealPage (Yardi)" as a single competitor. RealPage and Yardi are separate companies with separate products, separate antitrust postures (RealPage has a DOJ settlement; Yardi won summary judgment by proving its code couldn't share sensitive data), and separate market positions. This error would undermine credibility with a domain expert evaluator.

**Fix:** Split into two rows in any competitive landscape table displayed in the UI or documentation:

| Rentana | Fast setup, ML-based, proven pilot results | No MAB experiments, no action plans, no configurable client strategy |

Also update ROBOREV-ROADMAP.md itself to fix this.

### 1C. Narrative Tone and Specificity Audit

**Problem:** The narrative generated by Claude for the slideshow needs to sound like a senior revenue manager presenting to a VP of Operations — confident, specific, data-driven. Not like a chatbot hedging.

**Audit the Claude narrative prompts for these patterns and fix them:**

Bad patterns to eliminate:
- "It appears that..." → Replace with direct statements: "B1 is overpriced by $91 against every comp in the submarket."
- "You may want to consider..." → Replace with: "Cut B1 asking rent to $1,475 immediately."
- "This could potentially..." → Replace with specific projections: "At current velocity, B1 will hit 75% occupancy by mid-April."
- Generic observations without dollar amounts → Always include the revenue impact: "Every week of delay on B1 costs $1,778 in vacancy burn."
- Passive voice → Active: not "prices should be adjusted" but "reduce asking rent by $50"

Good patterns to ensure are present:
- B1 urgency: The narrative must communicate that $254/day is burning right now. Not future tense. Not conditional.
- A1 opportunity: High occupancy + approaching spring peak = "you have pricing power you're not using."
- A2 trajectory: "Occupancy has dropped 2 points per month for 3 consecutive months while you've raised asking rent $26. The market is telling you something."
- B2 puzzle framing: "The price is right — comps confirm it, executed rents confirm it. So why are 6 units sitting for 28 days? This needs investigation, not a price cut."

**Address the client directly:** "Your B1 units..." not "The B1 units..." — consulting tone, not academic tone.

**Verification:** Run the diagnostic. Read every slide's narrative aloud. If any sentence could appear in a generic report about any property, it's not specific enough. Every sentence should reference a specific number, unit type, or action.

## Category 2: Evaluator Experience Polish

### 2A. First-Load Experience Audit

**Problem:** An evaluator logs in for the first time. What do they see? Is it immediately clear what to do? Or do they stare at a dashboard wondering what to click?

**Audit and fix the following flow:**

1. **Login → Dashboard:** After login with demo@example.com / demo123, the dashboard should show a clear portfolio overview. Check that:
   - KPI cards are immediately readable (occupancy, vacancy cost, portfolio health)
   - There's an obvious visual hierarchy: the most important number (total vacancy burn: $911/day or $27,334/mo) should be prominent
   - It's obvious which property needs attention (B should visually stand out as having problems)
   - There's a clear call to action — not just data, but "what should I click next?"

2. **Dashboard → Property:** Clicking into a property should feel like drilling down, not starting over. The property view should:
   - Show the property's key metrics immediately (not require a tab click)
   - Have a prominent "Run AI Review" or "Get Diagnosis" button that's impossible to miss
   - Show the unit type breakdown with visual indicators of health (green/yellow/red or equivalent)

3. **Property → Diagnosis:** Running the diagnostic should:
   - Show clear loading state (not a blank screen for 12-15 seconds)
   - Progressive rendering: show metrics and flags immediately while Claude generates the narrative
   - After completion, the inline review should be the default view, with slideshow as an optional presentation mode
   - The diagnosis should be scannable — an evaluator should get the gist in 30 seconds, detail in 2 minutes

4. **Diagnosis → Slideshow:** The slideshow should:
   - Be accessible via a clear "Present as Slideshow" button (not hidden in a menu)
   - Have keyboard navigation (arrow keys) that works reliably
   - Property switching should work without re-running the diagnostic (if both properties have cached results)

**The 3-click test:** Login → Click property B → Click "Run Diagnosis" → See B1 flagged as critical with a clear action plan. If this takes more than 3 clicks or more than 20 seconds (including Claude processing), simplify.

### 2B. Loading and Progressive Rendering

**Problem:** 12–15 seconds of blank screen while Claude processes is an eternity for an evaluator.

**Fix:**
- Phase 1 (immediate, <1 second): Show all deterministic metrics (Layer 1 output). Occupancy, vacancy, exposure, asking rent, comp spread — these are computed in <10ms and should appear instantly.
- Phase 2 (immediate, <1 second): Show flags (Layer 1.5 output). Color-coded severity badges next to each unit type.
- Phase 3 (~3-5 seconds): Show Claude's scoring and diagnosis as it becomes available.
- Phase 4 (~5-12 seconds): Show narrative text and action plan.

The user should NEVER see a spinner with no content. At every moment during loading, there should be useful information on screen.

If progressive loading already exists, verify it works. If it's partially implemented, complete it.

### 2C. Visual Hierarchy and Information Density

**Problem:** Data-dense dashboards often fail because everything has equal visual weight.

**Audit each screen for:**

- **Primary number**: What's the ONE thing the evaluator should see first? Make it 2x the size of everything else.
  - Dashboard: Portfolio vacancy burn ($911/day)
  - Property A: A2 is the problem (86% occ, declining)
  - Property B: B1 is the crisis (79% occ, $91 above every comp)
  
- **Secondary context**: Supporting metrics that explain the primary number. Normal size, grouped near the primary.

- **Tertiary detail**: Everything else. Smaller, accessible but not competing for attention.

- **Color usage**: Red/warning colors should ONLY appear for CRITICAL and HIGH severity items. If everything is red, nothing is red. A1's healthy status should feel calm (green or neutral). B1's crisis should feel urgent.

- **Whitespace**: Dense tables are hard to scan. Add breathing room between sections. The evaluator should be able to distinguish "the healthy property" from "the problem property" at a glance without reading any numbers.

### 2D. Error States and Edge Cases

**Problem:** What happens when things go wrong? An evaluator who hits an error will form a strong negative impression.

**Test and fix these scenarios:**

1. **Claude API timeout:** Simulate by temporarily pointing at a bad endpoint or adding a very short timeout. The frontend should show a graceful fallback ("Analysis is taking longer than expected — showing metrics and flags while we retry") not a stack trace or blank screen.

2. **Claude returns malformed JSON:** Add a test case where the Claude client receives invalid JSON. The fallback template narrative should kick in, and the frontend should show a "Standard analysis" indicator as spec'd, not crash.

3. **Double-click on "Run Diagnosis":** If the evaluator clicks the button twice, it should not run two parallel diagnostics. Debounce or disable the button during execution.

4. **Browser back button:** If the evaluator hits back during a diagnostic run, then navigates forward, the state should be recoverable — not a blank screen requiring a re-run.

5. **Session expiry:** If the JWT expires during use (unlikely in a demo but possible), the redirect to login should be clean, not a 401 JSON blob.

## Category 3: Feature Extensions

### 3A. CSV Data Import

**Goal:** Let anyone try RoboRev with their own data. This is the bridge between "impressive demo" and "tool I can try with my portfolio."

**Build a CSV upload flow:**

1. **Upload UI:** Drag-and-drop zone on the dashboard or a dedicated "Import Data" page. Accept .csv and .xlsx files.

2. **Column mapping step:** After upload, show a mapping interface:
   - Auto-detect common column names (unit_type, beds, baths, asking_rent, occupancy, etc.)
   - Let the user manually map any unrecognized columns
   - Show a preview of the first 5 rows with the mapped columns
   - Validate: are required fields present? Are numeric fields actually numeric? Are percentages between 0 and 1 (or 0 and 100 — handle both)?

3. **Confirmation step:** Show a summary: "We found 3 unit types across 2 properties with 156 total units. Does this look right?" with a table showing the aggregated view (mimicking the EliseAI Pricing Export format).

4. **Import:** Create the property, unit types, and units in the database. Generate a default "Stabilized Balanced" config. Redirect to the new property's overview.

**Schema flexibility:** The import should handle:
- Column names with different casing (Asking_Rent, asking_rent, ASKING_RENT)
- Missing optional columns (demand_score, exposure breakdowns) — use sensible defaults or mark as unavailable
- Different date formats (MM/DD/YYYY, YYYY-MM-DD, DD-Mon-YY)
- Percentages as 0.96 or 96 — detect and normalize

**What it does NOT need to handle:** Comp data, historical snapshots, or lease-level detail. Those come later. The import creates a property with current-state metrics only.

### 3B. Configuration Templates Library

**Goal:** New users get a meaningful diagnosis in under 60 seconds by selecting a strategy template instead of manually tuning 40+ thresholds.

**Build 4 templates:**

1. **Stabilized Balanced** (default — already exists as the demo config)
   - Crisis below: 82% occupancy
   - Action threshold: 90% occupancy
   - Exposure crisis: 20%
   - Standard thresholds for a property in steady-state operation

2. **Value-Add Aggressive**
   - Crisis below: 72% occupancy (more tolerant — expects vacancy during renovations)
   - Wider price spreads tolerated (renovated units command premiums)
   - Higher amenity premium thresholds (renovated amenities justify larger premiums)
   - Shorter DOM tolerance (premium product should lease faster)

3. **Lease-Up**
   - Crisis below: 60% occupancy (brand new property, expects gradual fill)
   - Occupancy velocity is the primary metric, not occupancy level
   - Concession thresholds very permissive (concessions are expected during lease-up)
   - No renewal metrics (no tenants to renew yet)

4. **Affordable / Rent-Controlled**
   - Occupancy thresholds very tight (waitlists mean occupancy should be 97%+)
   - No comp-based pricing (rents are set by regulation, not market)
   - Exposure is the primary concern (every vacant day is lost revenue with no price lever)
   - Renewal focus is retention, not rent increase

**UI:** When creating a new config or during onboarding, show a card picker: "What best describes this property's strategy?" with the 4 templates. Each card has a 1-sentence description and shows 3-4 key threshold values. Selecting a template pre-fills all thresholds, which the user can then customize.

**Include a "Preview Diagnosis" button** that shows flag counts for the current property under the selected template BEFORE the user commits. This demonstrates the three-layer architecture's power: "Same data, different strategy, different diagnosis."

### 3C. AI Chat Interface

**Goal:** Let operators ask natural language questions about their pricing data. This directly mirrors EliseAI's product thesis (conversational AI for property management).

**Build a chat panel accessible from the property view:**

1. **Chat UI:** Collapsible side panel or bottom drawer. Persistent across property navigation. Simple input field + message history.

2. **Backend:** New endpoint `POST /api/chat` that:
   - Receives the user's question + current property context (property_id, latest metrics, latest flags, latest diagnosis if available)
   - Constructs a Claude prompt with the metrics as context
   - Returns Claude's response

3. **Claude prompt structure:**
   ```
   You are a senior revenue management analyst. You have access to the following 
   pricing metrics for {property_name}:
   
   {metrics_json — the Layer 1 output}
   
   {flags_json — the Layer 1.5 output}
   
   {diagnosis_summary — if a diagnosis has been run, include the scoring and key findings}
   
   The operator is asking: "{user_question}"
   
   Answer specifically with numbers from the metrics. Never invent numbers. 
   If the metrics don't contain the information needed to answer, say so and 
   suggest what data would be needed.
   
   Keep responses concise — 2-4 sentences for simple questions, up to a short 
   paragraph for complex ones. Use the same tone as a senior consultant in a 
   quick Slack exchange: direct, specific, no fluff.
   ```

4. **Suggested questions:** Show 3-4 clickable starter questions when the chat is empty:
   - "Why is B1 not leasing?"
   - "What happens if I cut B2 by $50?"
   - "Which unit type needs attention first?"
   - "What's my biggest revenue risk right now?"

5. **Key constraint:** The chat must NEVER compute dollar amounts — it can only reference amounts that exist in the metrics JSON. If the user asks "what if I cut rent by $50," Claude can reason about directional impact and reference existing vacancy burn numbers, but cannot fabricate projected savings.

### 3D. PDF Export of Diagnostic Results

**Goal:** Operators need to share diagnostic results with asset managers and investors who will never log into the platform.

**Build a "Download Report" button on the diagnosis view that generates a PDF containing:**

1. **Header:** Property name, date, RoboRev branding
2. **Executive summary:** 3-4 sentences covering overall health, top concern, top opportunity
3. **Unit type scorecards:** For each unit type — health score, grade, key metrics (occupancy, asking, comps, exposure), top 3 flags
4. **Action plan summary:** Phase 1 actions with revenue impact estimates
5. **Appendix:** Full metrics table, flag list, comp data

**Use a Python PDF library** (reportlab or weasyprint) on the backend. The endpoint returns a PDF file. Frontend triggers download.

**Keep it simple:** This is a data export, not a designed document. Clean tables, readable fonts, company header. No charts in V1 (charts in PDF are a pain — save for later).
